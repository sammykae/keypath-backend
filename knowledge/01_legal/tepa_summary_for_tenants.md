# TEPA Summary for Tenants  
### Tokenized Equity Participation Agreement (Plain Language Version)

This document explains the TEPA — the Tokenized Equity Participation Agreement — in clear, everyday language. The TEPA is the agreement that governs how you earn and hold “Tokens,” which represent economic value in the entity that owns your rental home.

This summary is for comprehension only. Your actual legally binding TEPA may include additional details.

---

# 1. What the TEPA Is — and What It Is NOT

**The TEPA gives renters a way to build equity and wealth while renting.  
It does NOT make you a homeowner or place you on the property deed.**

### Tokens ARE:
- A way to build economic value tied to the property.  
- Earned through on-time rent and monthly contributions.  
- Purchasable in small amounts over time.  
- Redeemable for cash when you move out or exit the program.  
- Protected from dilution (your share cannot shrink).  
- Safe from landlord refinancing, liens, or new investors.

### Tokens are NOT:
- Title or deed to the home  
- A mortgage  
- A loan  
- A rental discount  
- Voting rights or control over the property  
- A form of co-ownership

The **Lease** controls your tenancy (rent, repairs, eviction rules).  
The **TEPA** controls your Token rights only.

---

# 2. How You Earn Tokens

You can earn Tokens in three primary ways:

### 2.1 Monthly Contributions
You may choose to contribute a specific monthly amount (ex: $100–$500).  
These convert into Tokens at the property’s current Token Value.

### 2.2 On-Time Rent Behavior
Certain TEPA versions include “good-care incentives,” such as:
- Consistent on-time rent for 3, 6, or 12 months
- Lease renewal
- Completing maintenance tasks (if your landlord enables them)
- Uploading photos after certain alerts (humidity, filters, etc.)

These may earn partial Tokens as rewards.

### 2.3 Spot Payments
You may make one-time contributions (“spot payments”).  
These Tokens usually **vest immediately** — meaning they cannot be forfeited.

---

# 3. What Is Token Value?

Token Value represents the current value of each Token.  
It updates once per year during the **Annual Valuation**.

**Token Value may go up or down.**  
If it goes down:  
➡️ You never owe money.  
➡️ Your Tokens simply become worth less for that year.

---

# 4. Annual Valuation — Once Per Year

Each year (typically March 1–31), the landlord and tenant select a valuation method:

- Independent Appraisal  
- Broker Price Opinion (BPO)  
- Automated Valuation Model (AVM blend: Zillow, Redfin, CoreLogic)  

Valuation determines:

- Updated property value  
- Updated Token Value  
- Whether new Tokens need to be minted  
- Updates to tenant and landlord shares

KeyPath does not pay for the valuation.  
Costs are typically shared or handled per TEPA terms.

---

# 5. Vested vs. Unvested Tokens

### Vested Tokens:
- Fully earned  
- Cannot be taken away  
- Paid out at full value when you exit

### Unvested Tokens may be forfeited if:
- Rent goes unpaid  
- Lease rules are violated  
- Property damage occurs  
- You are lawfully evicted

The TEPA will specify the vesting schedule.

---

# 6. Good Standing Requirements

To earn new Tokens, you must be in **Good Standing**:

- Rent is paid  
- No major lease violations  
- No serious property damage  
- No unauthorized occupants  
- No illegal subletting  

If you fall out of Good Standing:
- Token accrual pauses  
- Unvested Tokens may be at risk

---

# 7. Protections for Tenants

The TEPA includes several important protections.

### 7.1 Anti-Dilution
Your percentage of the Tenant Token Pool **cannot shrink** due to:
- Refinancing  
- New debt  
- HELOCs  
- New landlords or new investors  
- Changes to the landlord’s capital stack  

### 7.2 Refinance Safety Firewall
If the landlord refinances:
- Your Tokens stay intact  
- Your Token Value remains based on property value, not debt  
- New lender relationships do not affect your equity

### 7.3 Mortgage, Tax, or Insurance Delinquency
Landlord must notify you within 10 business days if serious issues arise.

You may receive:
- The right to “cure” (pay to fix the delinquency temporarily)  
- Priority repayment if you choose to cure  
- Bonus Tokens for curing  
- Protection in foreclosure (you get paid first)

### 7.4 Habitability and Repair Rules
If the landlord fails to maintain the property:
- You may pause contributions  
- You may receive compensation Tokens  
- You may exit with full-value payout

These vary by TEPA version.

---

# 8. Exiting the Program (Move-Out)

When you move out, you receive:

**Cash-Out = Vested Tokens × Token Value**

Minus:
- Unpaid rent  
- Damages  
- Cleaning / turnover fees  

Unvested Tokens return to the landlord.  
You may not owe anything beyond normal lease obligations.

---

# 9. Selling Tokens Before Move-Out

Depending on your TEPA:

1. **Landlord has First Right of Refusal**  
2. If landlord declines:
   - You may sell Tokens to approved investors  
3. If no buyer is found:
   - You may sell Tokens back to KeyPath or its partners at a preset percentage of Token Value  

Outside trading is not allowed.

---

# 10. Buying the Home

If you reach a high ownership threshold (often 80%+ of the Tenant Token Pool):

- You may begin a purchase process  
- You may convert remaining value into a down payment  
- You may qualify for a deed transfer if both parties agree  

Deed transfer is handled with a **title company or attorney**, not KeyPath.

---

# 11. Final Notes

- The Lease controls tenancy and landlord–tenant law.  
- The TEPA controls Token rights.  
- Tokens never equal homeownership or deed rights.  
- KeyPath is a neutral recordkeeper — not the landlord, not a lender, not an escrow agent.
